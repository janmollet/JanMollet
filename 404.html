Jan Mollet Molina

I am Jan Mollet Molina, and I am passionate about using data and technology to make cities smarter, more efficient, and sustainable. With a background in Smart Cities and Urban Informatics, I focus on developing data-driven solutions that tackle urban challenges like transportation, infrastructure, and accessibility.

Cities face a range of complex problems, from traffic congestion and outdated systems to environmental issues and social inequality. I believe technology has the potential to solve these problems by making cities more connected, efficient, and inclusive. My work revolves around using data science, smart tech, and GIS to improve how cities function and enhance the lives of their residents.

I specialize in urban mobility, sustainable infrastructure, and inclusive design, always with the goal of making urban spaces more livable, resilient, and equitable. Whether it's optimizing transportation or designing smarter, greener cities, I am dedicated to driving positive change through technology and data.

I am excited to be part of building the cities of the future — ones that are smarter, more sustainable, and work better for everyone.

Fun fact: I have been playing the saxophone since I was 3 years old! Check out the music section.

My Music Journey

Music is my favourite hobby. I love listening to music, but I also love playing it. I started music lessons when I was three years old, learning music theory and trying out different instruments. At five, I chose my first instrument, the clarinet. Although it's a beautiful instrument, the clarinet isn't very popular among kids, who often go for the piano, drums, or guitar. It's mostly associated with classical music, but it can also be played in genres like jazz.

At ten years old, after five years of playing the clarinet, I had a moment of doubt. Many of my friends were quitting their music lessons, and I wasn’t sure whether to continue. Fortunately, my parents, Sabina Molina and Jordi Mollet, encouraged me to stick with it and my dad even joined me by picking up the tenor saxophone. Watching him play inspired me, and I eventually switched to the alto saxophone, which is much more versatile and can be played across many musical styles.

At sixteen, due to the demands of high school and soccer (which I also love), I didn’t have enough time to dedicate to music and ended up quitting. Still, the desire to return to playing and join a band never left me—a childhood dream I’ve always kept alive.

As if destined, one day my friend Raúl Fernandez, a talented guitarist, asked me to join his band, Police Line Band, a well-known local group that played at various venues. I had the incredible opportunity to perform with them for nearly six years, an unforgettable experience. Over time, I became more confident on stage, overcoming my initial shyness, and significantly improved my technique and sound.

Watching the band grow was amazing. We went from small gigs to larger performances and eventually started earning money from our music. What made it truly special was the bond we shared, not just as bandmates but as family. We supported one another, celebrated every milestone, and grew together as musicians and friends.

While playing with the band, I also launched my own projects. I recorded my first single, Sa Brava (with a video filmed on the Costa Brava in Spain), and my first EP, Flavour EP (five songs), with my friend and producer Jan Adlan. Thanks to all my prior experience, I began performing solo at restaurants, weddings, and private events.

I used various platforms to promote myself as a musician, such as Instagram, websites, and even apps. It became a small business that allowed me to do what I loved: play music while also earning an income. I was surprised at how well it went. Some weeks, I had more than two gigs. It was the perfect side activity while I was still studying at university.

After finishing school, since I couldn’t study abroad for a semester, I decided to leave everything behind in Spain and spend a year living in the U.S. as an Au Pair.

Of course, I couldn’t leave without my saxophone. I started researching local bands in the area where I was staying (Connecticut) and ended up joining One Bad Oyster for a full year. Thanks to that experience, I met Mars Hendrik, who came to one of our shows and later invited me to join his band and record music for his new album. It was a dream come true to record in a New York studio. During that time, I also released my second single from the U.S., Copacabana Heights.

Now, the first song of Mars Hendrik's upcoming album, Karma, is already out. The full album will be available on all major music platforms by summer/fall 2025. It was recorded at Black Barrel Records, a label based in Shelton, Connecticut, which signed Mars as their main artist. Black Barrel manages his tours, releases, and promotions.
